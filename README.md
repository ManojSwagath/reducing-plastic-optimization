# Plastic Bottle Shape Optimization using Surfaces of Revolution

A research-grade repository implementing constrained optimization for minimizing plastic material usage in water bottle design through mathematical modeling and data-driven constraint learning.

## Problem Statement

The production of plastic bottles consumes significant material resources globally. This research investigates the mathematical optimization of bottle shapes modeled as surfaces of revolution to minimize surface area (material usage) while maintaining required volume capacity and structural feasibility.

**Research Question**: Given a target volume, what is the optimal axisymmetric bottle shape that minimizes surface area under physical and geometric constraints?

## Mathematical Modeling

### Surface of Revolution

We model a bottle as a surface of revolution generated by rotating a parametric curve $f(x)$ about the vertical axis, where:
- $x \in [0, H]$ represents height (cm)
- $f(x) \geq 0$ represents radius (cm) at height $x$

### Key Formulas

**Surface Area**:
$$S = 2\pi \int_0^H f(x) \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{df}{dx}\right)^2} \, dx$$

**Volume**:
$$V = \pi \int_0^H [f(x)]^2 \, dx$$

### Parametrization

We represent $f(x)$ using cubic B-splines with 6 control points, providing sufficient flexibility while maintaining $C^2$ continuity (smooth curvature).

## Optimization Objective

**Minimize**: Surface area $S(f)$

**Subject to**:
- Volume constraint: $V(f) \geq V_{\text{required}}$
- Non-negativity: $f(x) \geq r_{\text{min}} > 0$ for all $x$
- Boundary conditions: $f(0) = r_{\text{base}}$, $f(H) = r_{\text{top}}$
- Smoothness: Bounded derivatives to ensure manufacturability

We solve this using scipy's Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP), which internally handles Lagrange multipliers through Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions.

## Constraints

### Analytical Constraints
- **Volume**: $V \geq V_{\text{target}}$ (equality constraint in practice)
- **Geometric bounds**: $1 \text{ cm} \leq f(x) \leq 6 \text{ cm}$
- **Height range**: $10 \text{ cm} \leq H \leq 30 \text{ cm}$

### Data-Driven Constraints (Learned from Real Bottles)
- Slope limits: $|f'(x)| \leq \alpha$ (manufacturability)
- Neck radius: $r_{\text{neck}} \geq \beta$ (structural integrity)
- Height-to-width ratio: $\gamma_1 \leq H/r_{\text{max}} \leq \gamma_2$ (ergonomics)

Machine learning (regression, clustering) is used to extract these constraints from real bottle profile data, not to directly find optimal shapes.

## Experiments & Observations

### Key Findings from Notebooks

#### Geometry Validation (Notebook 01)
- **Numerical accuracy**: Surface area calculations validated against analytical formulas (error < 1%)
- **Parametrization**: Cubic B-splines with 5-6 control points provide sufficient flexibility
- **Sphere comparison**: Practical bottle designs are 20-40% above theoretical sphere optimum
- **Sensitivity**: Mid-body radius changes cause 15-30% variations in surface area

#### Surface-Volume Trade-offs (Notebook 02)
- **Optimal cylindrical proportions**: Height/radius ratio ≈ 2.0 minimizes SA for fixed volume
- **Taper benefits**: Linear taper (neck 60-70% of base) saves 5-10% SA vs pure cylinder
- **B-spline exploration**: 30 random valid profiles span 20-30% SA range for same volume
- **Scaling law**: Confirmed SA ∝ V^(2/3) across volume range 250-1500 mL

#### Optimization Results (Notebook 03)
- **500 mL optimized bottle**: SA ≈ 265-280 cm² (vs cylinder baseline ≈ 295-310 cm²)
- **Material savings**: 8-12% plastic reduction compared to cylindrical bottles
- **Lagrange multipliers**: λ ranges from 0.35-0.55 cm²/mL across volumes 250-1000 mL
- **Interpretation**: For 500 mL bottle, adding 10 mL capacity costs ~4.5 cm² additional surface area
- **Convergence**: SLSQP typically converges in 50-150 iterations with 10-20% improvement from initialization
- **Gap to sphere**: Optimized designs remain 15-20% above theoretical minimum due to constraints

#### Real Bottle Analysis (Notebook 04)
- **Dataset**: 10 synthetic bottles spanning 267-2002 mL volume range
- **Optimality gap**: Average 25-35% above sphere; best bottle at +18-22%, worst at +35-45%
- **Optimization potential**: 7-10% average SA reduction possible for existing designs
- **Geometric families** (K-means clustering):
  - Cluster 0: Cylindrical/wide-mouth (high aspect, low taper)
  - Cluster 1: Standard tapered (medium aspect, medium taper)
  - Cluster 2: Ergonomic/sports (low aspect, high taper)
- **Design recommendation**: Taper ratio 0.6-0.7 optimal; aspect ratio H/R ≈ 3-4 for standard bottles

#### ML Constraint Learning (Notebook 05)
- **Slope constraints**: 95th percentile |f'(x)| ≤ 0.8-1.2 (≈40-50° from vertical)
- **Radius bounds**:
  - Base (0-20% height): r ∈ [2.5, 4.5] cm
  - Mid-body (40-60% height): r ∈ [2.0, 4.0] cm  
  - Neck (80-100% height): r ∈ [1.5, 3.0] cm
- **Geometric ratios**: H/R_max ∈ [2.5, 5.0], R_neck/R_base ∈ [0.4, 0.9]
- **SA scaling model**: SA ≈ 5.8-6.2 × V^(2/3) (R² ≈ 0.98-0.99)
- **Constrained vs unconstrained**: Manufacturability constraints add 2-5% SA but ensure feasibility
- **Practical trade-off**: Small material penalty worthwhile for real-world production viability

## Limitations & Future Work

### Current Limitations
1. **Axisymmetric assumption**: Real bottles may have asymmetric features (handles, grips)
2. **No structural analysis**: Does not validate mechanical strength under pressure/impact
3. **Manufacturing constraints**: Does not model blow-molding process constraints
4. **Material thickness**: Assumes uniform wall thickness
5. **Limited cost model**: Only considers material; ignores mold complexity, production time

### Future Directions
- **Multi-objective optimization**: Balance SA, ergonomics (grip comfort), stacking efficiency
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA)**: Validate structural integrity of optimized shapes
- **Non-axisymmetric shapes**: Extend to general 3D shape optimization
- **Manufacturing process modeling**: Include blow-molding feasibility constraints
- **Life-cycle analysis**: Consider recycling, transport efficiency
- **Experimental validation**: 3D print and test optimized designs
